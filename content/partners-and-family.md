# For Partners and Family

When someone you love has Endometriosis or Adenomyosis, it affects the whole relationship. You don't need medical training to make a difference - showing up, listening, and learning are the most powerful things you can do.

## What to say

| Instead of this | Try this |
|---|---|
| "It's just a bad period" | "I can see this is more than cramps. Tell me what you need." |
| "Have you tried yoga/supplements?" | "What has your specialist recommended?" |
| "At least it's not cancer" | "Chronic pain is serious. I'm sorry you're dealing with this." |
| "You don't look sick" | "I know some days are harder than others, even when it doesn't show." |
| "Just push through it" | "It's okay to rest. I'll handle things today." |

## How to help during flares

A flare can mean hours or days of debilitating pain, fatigue, and nausea. Here's what actually helps:

- **Don't ask "what can I do?"** - offer specifics: "I'm making dinner tonight" or "I'll pick up the kids"
- **Heat helps** - have heating pads or a hot water bottle ready
- **Reduce sensory input** - dim lights, lower noise, keep the space calm
- **Handle logistics** - call in sick for them, reschedule appointments, manage the household
- **Don't take it personally** - pain can cause irritability, withdrawal, or crying. It's not about you
- **Track patterns together** - use the [Symptom Tracker](/tracker/) to help identify triggers and cycles

## Understand the condition

Endometriosis and Adenomyosis are not "bad periods." Key facts for supporters:

- **It's chronic** - there is no cure. Treatment manages symptoms but doesn't eliminate them
- **Pain doesn't correlate with severity** - Stage I can be as painful as Stage IV
- **Fatigue is real** - chronic inflammation causes bone-deep exhaustion, not just tiredness
- **It affects everything** - work, sex, fertility, mental health, social life, finances
- **Diagnosis takes 7-10 years on average** - your loved one may have been dismissed by doctors for years
- **Surgery is not a fix-all** - even after excision, symptoms can persist or recur

For a full overview, see [Endometriosis](/endometriosis/) and [Adenomyosis](/adenomyosis/).

## Supporting their medical journey

- **Go to appointments** - a second set of ears helps, especially with complex information
- **Help them prepare** - review their [symptom tracker data](/tracker/) together before visits
- **Advocate** - if a doctor dismisses their pain, support them in seeking a second opinion
- **Research together** - use [Find a Specialist](/find-a-specialist/) to find excision surgeons
- **Understand treatment choices** - read [Treatments](/treatments/) so you can discuss options as a team

## For partners specifically

Endometriosis can strain intimate relationships:

- **Pain during sex (dyspareunia)** affects many patients. This is a medical symptom, not rejection
- **Communicate openly** about what feels okay and what doesn't. Intimacy takes many forms
- **Fertility concerns** are common. If relevant, explore options together - see [Fertility](/fertility/)
- **Your feelings matter too** - it's okay to feel frustrated, helpless, or grieving. Seek your own support

## Conversation script

Use this when explaining the condition to someone who doesn't understand:

> I've been reading about Endometriosis. The pattern of pain I've been describing matches what's documented for this condition. It's not "just bad cramps." It's an inflammatory condition that grows tissue similar to the uterine lining in places it doesn't belong. I'd like your support in pursuing a proper diagnosis.

More scripts for doctors, social media, and workplace conversations are on [Take Action](/take-action/).

## Take care of yourself

Supporting someone with a chronic illness is demanding. You can't pour from an empty cup:

- **Set boundaries** - you can be supportive without sacrificing your own health
- **Talk to someone** - a therapist or support group helps process caregiver stress
- **Stay connected** - maintain your own friendships and interests
- **Acknowledge your feelings** - frustration, helplessness, and grief are normal

For mental health resources, see [Mental Health](/mental-health/).
