# Find a Specialist

Getting the right diagnosis and treatment for Endometriosis or Adenomyosis often depends on finding a specialist who understands these conditions. The average delay from symptom onset to diagnosis is **7-10 years**, largely because many general OB/GYNs receive limited training on Endometriosis.

## Why a specialist matters

- General gynecologists may miss or dismiss symptoms
- Endometriosis requires surgical expertise (excision, not ablation) for effective treatment
- Adenomyosis can be misdiagnosed as fibroids or heavy periods
- Specialists are more likely to use up-to-date diagnostic and treatment approaches

## What to look for in a specialist

| Quality | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Excision training** | Excision (cutting out lesions) is the gold standard. Ablation (burning) has higher recurrence rates |
| **High case volume** | Surgeons who treat Endometriosis regularly have better outcomes |
| **Multidisciplinary approach** | Complex cases may involve colorectal, urological, or thoracic surgeons |
| **Willing to listen** | A good specialist takes your symptoms seriously and doesn't dismiss your pain |
| **Transparent about outcomes** | Ask about complication rates, recurrence rates, and recovery timelines |

## Types of specialists

- **Excision surgeon** - Specializes in laparoscopic excision of Endometriosis. This is the most important specialist for surgical treatment
- **Reproductive endocrinologist (RE)** - Focuses on fertility; helpful if you're trying to conceive with Endometriosis or Adenomyosis
- **Pelvic pain specialist** - Manages chronic pelvic pain, often using a multimodal approach
- **Minimally invasive gynecologic surgeon (MIGS)** - Trained in advanced laparoscopic and robotic techniques
- **Adenomyosis specialist** - Some gynecologists specialize in Adenomyosis diagnosis via MRI and advanced ultrasound

## Questions to ask a potential specialist

Use these questions when evaluating a new doctor. You can also bring this list to your appointment.

> **About their experience:**
> - How many Endometriosis/Adenomyosis surgeries do you perform per year?
> - Do you perform excision or ablation?
> - Do you operate on deep infiltrating Endometriosis (DIE)?
> - What is your approach to multidisciplinary cases (bowel, bladder, diaphragm)?
>
> **About your care:**
> - What diagnostic approach do you use before recommending surgery?
> - How do you manage pain while awaiting surgery?
> - Do you work with pelvic floor physiotherapists?
> - What is the typical recovery timeline?
>
> **Red flags to watch for:**
> - "You're too young to have Endometriosis"
> - Recommending hysterectomy as a first-line treatment for Endometriosis
> - Dismissing symptoms as "normal period pain"
> - Unwilling to discuss surgical options or refer to a specialist
